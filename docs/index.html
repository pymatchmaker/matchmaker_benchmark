How well can a machine follow a live performer through a score? Every entry below was measured on the same frozen set of performances, by the same evaluation code.

Tracked

A piece counts as tracked unless two or more 30-second segments drift past the tolerance — 0.5 beats for MIDI, 1.0 for audio. A follower that loses the performer produces meaningless errors, so accuracy is only reported over the pieces it held onto.

Why medians read 0.000

On MIDI the ground truth is note onsets and a follower reports score onsets, so more than half of all events land exactly right and the median collapses to zero. Beat err (mean) and ≤ 0.3 beat are what separate the methods there.

Beat err · all

The median error over every piece, lost ones included. It keeps a follower from looking flawless by tracking two easy pieces and abandoning the rest.

RTF

Processing time divided by performance duration. Anything at or above 1.0 cannot keep up with a live performer, however accurate it is offline.

Per-piece and per-dataset results

Opening a row loads its full record: every pooled metric for each dataset and for the whole fold, a table of all 146 pieces — or of one dataset's, by clicking it — and the alignment of any piece against the ground truth. The plots are drawn here from the raw path coordinates — no images are stored.

Reference rows

Entries marked reference are the methods that ship with matchmaker, run on the same fold by the same code. They are what a new score follower is trying to beat, not competitors.
